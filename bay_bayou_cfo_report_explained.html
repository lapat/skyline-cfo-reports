📖 Page-by-Page Guide to Jim's 10-Page Report

Here is the intuitive, plain-English explanation of every page in `BAYBAYOUFINANCIALSTMTS63026.pdf`:

Page 1: Comparative Balance Sheet (June 30, 2026 vs June 30, 2025) Balance Sheet

What it tells us: What Bay Bayou owns (Assets), what it owes (Liabilities), and what Jay & partners actually own free-and-clear (Members' Equity).

  • Property & Equipment ($5,931,797 net): Land is carried at cost ($2.17M). Site improvements ($4.42M) and buildings ($2.11M) are depreciated down by $4.00M in accumulated depreciation.
  • Cash ($1,873,702): Liquid money sitting across Bay Bayou's operating and payroll accounts at Fifth Third Bank.
  • Goodwill ($3,125,369): Premium value recognized upon acquisition representing the park's brand, waterfront location, and established reputation.
  • Long-Term Debt ($7,901,376): The Byline Bank mortgage. It originated at $8.5M and has been paid down from $8.07M last year, proving steady principal reduction.
  • Members' Equity ($2,646,555): The net worth of the partnership. Reconciles 100% to ending equity on Page 2!
Page 2: Statement of Income & Retained Earnings P&L

What it tells us: The official profit waterfall from top-line sales to bottom-line net income.

  • Total Revenue ($1,931,607): Rental leases from existing sites ($1.36M) and expansion sites ($411.6K), plus electricity ($96.8K), laundry ($24.5K), and propane ($10.5K).
  • Operating Expenses ($1,112,000): Pure day-to-day property operating costs (payroll, landscaping, utilities, management fees).
  • Operating Income / NOI ($819,607): The true earnings engine. 42.43% of every dollar taken in falls to NOI!
  • Financing Deductions: Mortgage interest ($266,776) and non-cash depreciation ($161,640) deduct $433,986 from NOI.
  • Net Income ($385,621): Taxable profit added to Beginning Equity ($2,260,934) to arrive at Ending Equity ($2,646,555).
Pages 3 & 4: Detail by Month (Jan – Jun 2026) Seasonality Matrix

What it tells us: The heartbeat of the property month by month.

Notice the revenue breakdown by contract duration: Daily, Weekly, Monthly, and 2–6 Month Snowbird contracts. In winter (Jan–March), 4-month contracts bring in $280K and monthly brings in $433K. In summer (May–June), 4-month drops to $0, and the park relies on annual residents ($281K/yr) and short-term transient guests.

Page 5: Operating Expense Comparative Analysis (% of Revenue) Cost Discipline

What it tells us: Cost control benchmarking. Every line is measured against total revenue:

  • Management Fees: Exactly 11.75% ($226,964). This is the contractual fee paid to affiliated Jennings Realty.
  • Utilities (Water & Electric): 9.88% ($190,835).
  • Total Payroll (Maintenance + Office): 12.76% ($246,406).
  • Repairs & Maintenance: 1.83% ($35,443) — down sharply from 3.15% in 2025!
Pages 6, 7 & 8: Occupancy & Length-of-Stay Reports Guest Analytics

What it tells us: Where the guest nights came from across the 308 sites:

Annual guests generate 8,861 site nights (25% of occupancy), 4-month snowbirds generate 1,446 nights, and monthly guests generate 5,338 nights. Total rented site nights: 32,920 nights (58.73% 6-month average occupancy).

Page 9: ADR & RevPAR (Hospitality Unit Economics) Unit Economics

ADR (Average Daily Rate): The average revenue collected per occupied night: $56.85 in 2026 vs. $53.27 in 2025. Rates increased by +$3.58/night!

RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Site): Total rental revenue divided by total site capacity: $33.39 in 2026 vs. $35.50 in 2025. Slightly lower because early spring had more vacant sites due to bad weather.

Page 10: Utility Detail & Cost Recovery Analysis Utility Submetering

What it tells us: How effectively the park passes through electricity costs to guests:

  • Total Electricity Bill paid to TECO: $130,503.49
  • Electric Revenue billed back to guests via site submeters: $96,784.00
  • Electric Recovery Ratio: 74.2% (meaning the park successfully recaptured nearly 3 out of every 4 dollars spent on power!).
